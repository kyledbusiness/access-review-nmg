# Access Review for Northstar Medical Group

## Problem Statement
- Northstar Medical Group has 48 users across HR, IT, Operations, and Finance departments. Employees have accumulated access to shared resources, business applications, and administrative groups over time, but the organization lacks a consistent way to verify whether those permissions are still necessary.
    - Users may retain access from previous responsibilities or expired contracts.
    - Sensitive or privileged permissions may lack a clear business justification.
    - There is no consolidated record showing which access should be kept, removed, revalidated, or disabled and who should approve those decisions.

**The challenge:** Determine which permissions are justified by each user's current role, identify access that creates unnecessary risk, and document a defensible path to remediation.

## Solution Overview
- I built a simulated access review for 48 users across four departments, comparing job responsibilities and business justification against assigned groups and permissions. I classified findings, documented the associated risks, recommended whether access should be kept, removed, revalidated, or disabled, and identified the appropriate decision owner.

The goal was to apply least-privilege thinking while making each recommendation traceable to a business need, risk, and approval.

## Key Achievements
- Built a structured access-review register for 48 users across four departments, linking each access assignment to a role, business need, finding, and recommended action.
- Worked through realistic access scenarios involving outdated permissions, expired contractors, and privileged accounts to determine when access should be kept, removed, or revalidated.
- Documented the reasoning and appropriate decision owners for each recommendation, turning a list of permissions into a review process that supports accountability and least-privilege decisions.
